# Heart Rate and Breathing Effects on Attention and Memory (HeartBEAM)

Aging is associated with autonomic imbalance, characterized by increased sympathetic and decreased parasympathetic activity, which has been linked to elevated plasma amyloid-β (Aβ) and increased Alzheimer's disease risk, as well as to impaired attention and memory function. The current project investigated whether a slow-paced breathing intervention combined with heart rate variability (HRV) biofeedback, aimed at enhancing parasympathetic activity and increasing heart rate oscillations, would benefit brain structure and function, cognition, and Aβ-related biomarkers in older adults. Primary and secondary hypotheses concerned plasma Aβ levels, perivascular space volume, and hippocampal volume. The study also included EEG recordings during an auditory oddball task (assessing distractor-evoked P3 amplitude) and structural MRI, allowing examination of relationships between attention/memory-related neural processing and brain structure over the course of the intervention.

#### Study design: Study Type: Interventional (Clinical Trial) Actual Enrollment: 91 participants (62 randomized: 31 slow-paced, 31 random-paced) Sessions: 5 campus lab visits (2 EEG and neuropsychological assessment visits at pre- and post-intervention, and 3 MRI visits at pre-, mid-, and post-intervention), plus 2 weeks of at-home cognitive, emotion, and sleep assessments (Week 1 and Week 12) Allocation: Randomized Intervention Model: Parallel Assignment Intervention Model Description: Participants were randomly assigned to a slow-paced breathing condition (breathing paces of 10, 12, and 15 s/breath) or a random-paced breathing control condition (breathing paces of 4, 5, and 6 s/breath). Both groups completed two daily 15-minute HRV-biofeedback breathing sessions for 10 weeks using an ear-clip PPG sensor and a laptop-based application. 
Study registered on ClinicalTrials.gov: https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05602220
